# Why Loam exists

## The short version

Making a game alone, or in a tiny team, means holding a lot in your head: what the game is supposed to feel like, why you made the choices you made, the references that inspired it, and the skills you are still learning. That is usually the first thing to get lost, in a pile of screenshots, in notes scattered across apps, in decisions nobody remembers making.

Loam is a free, open-source desktop app for indie developers who want to make games creatively and artistically. It keeps your references, your game's intent and your learning in one place, as files you own, so you can keep making the game you meant to make.

## What it helps with

- **Collect and arrange references.** A library with tags and search, and canvas boards with groups and notes.
- **Write down the game's intent.** A creative bible: design pillars, world rules, places, creatures, characters, a decision log and an idea parking lot, all checked against your pillars.
- **See yourself learn.** A study journal with sessions, topics and side-by-side comparison of your practice work over time.
- **Optional helpers.** Tag suggestions and study recaps from a small model that runs on your own computer. Off by default; nothing is applied without your click.

## Principles

1. **Your vision decides.** Loam keeps track of what you want the game to be. It never decides for you.
2. **Your files, your machine.** Bibles and journals are plain Markdown folders you can open in any editor, put in git and share with a teammate. Nothing leaves your computer unless you explicitly ask.
3. **Start small, grow with the game.** No 40-page design document up front. One question to start: _What should players feel?_
4. **Never punish.** No streaks, no guilt. Learning and making games is hard enough.
5. **Indie first.** Built for solo developers and small teams, not studio pipelines.

## Who makes it

Loam is made by a solo indie developer in Korea, as a tool for their own game first and then for anyone it helps.

## Where it is going

- **Now:** references, canvas boards, creative bible, study journal, optional local helpers.
- **Next:** better ways to keep a game and its bible in step as the game grows, and closer links to game engines.
- **Later:** community-made starter kits and learning-resource lists.

Feedback from indie developers decides what comes next.
